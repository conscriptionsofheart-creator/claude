You are rebuilding the sculpting engine of Ruth's headset app so she can sculpt objects in VR as well as in Adobe Substance 3D Modeler, with the features Substance has. Read README.md first, then research/SUBSTANCE_TOOLS_AND_HOW_THEY_WORK.md, research/bst_sculpt_in_vr_program_and_tool_words.md and research/bst_sculpt_plan.html, then the code in scripts/.

WHO AND HOW
- Ruth Diaz sculpts with her hands on a Meta Quest (WebXR, hand tracking and controllers) inside two web pages, the Atelier and the BSA. She does not use Blender and wants tools she can use, not templates to assemble. She has no money: free and open source only, nothing paid, no subscriptions. Address her as "you".
- Hard rules: never hand her a VR scene under 20 fps (aim 72); no flashing animations; use the Atelier's own look for any UI (no browser dialogs); every visible tool gets a one-step lesson; people and characters keep their full design (only props may be thinned); sculpting must never break a figure's skeleton, skin weights or shape keys.

WHAT TO BUILD
1. A new engine that replaces scripts/bst_vr_sculpt.js behind the SAME interface (scripts/sculpt_next_README.md lists it). Design it for objects as well as figures. Aim at Substance 3D Modeler's feature set as listed in the research file: clay add/subtract/paint/intersect on every brush; Clay, Smooth, Flatten, Pinch/Crease, Inflate, Grab/Move with falloff, Scrape, Stamp-along-stroke, Smear; primitives with a transform gizmo and boolean modes; a cutter (a saw that cuts only the width of the blade, sweeping while the pinch is held, already in bst_cut.js/bst_vr_sculpt.js, keep it); mirror and radial symmetry on every tool; layers and groups with undo; stroke stabiliser; two-hand grab to move and scale the whole work; remesh/decimate to a triangle budget; export glTF.
2. Pick an internal representation that fits a Quest in a browser (for example adaptive voxel/SDF near the hand meshed in a Web Worker, or dynamic-topology triangle sculpting), state your choice and the measured cost per stroke on a phone-class GPU, and keep the figure path (skinned meshes, shape keys) working. Do not rewrite what works: reuse bst_cut.js (cut, carve, stamp, heal), the clothes cut saving in wardrobe_core.js, and the wrist menu code in vr_core.js (sculptRows, sculptPieceRows, Touches: clothes and body, Engine tile).
3. Deliver it as a drop-in: the new engine file(s) named sculpt_next_engine.js and sculpt_next_*.js (the server serves one flat folder, no sub-folders) exporting createVrSculpt(THREE, core, host) and optionally makeSculpt(THREE). Ruth plugs it in by replacing scripts/sculpt_next_engine.js in the app's lib folder, then in the headset: Sculpt > More tools > "Engine: next", leave Sculpt, enter again. If the file fails to load the app falls back to the current engine by itself.
4. Also deliver: a self-test page that runs without a headset (extend scripts/bst_vr_sculpt_selftest.html), a short list of every new wrist-menu tile with its picture-tile icon name, and one lesson step per tool.

HOW TO WORK
- Work in this order and stop at a working slice each time: (a) clay brush plus smooth with add/subtract on a sphere, symmetry; (b) primitives, gizmo, booleans, cutter; (c) layers, undo; (d) figures: confirm skin weights and shape keys survive; (e) remesh, decimate, export.
- Measure before claiming: triangle counts, ms per stroke, fps in the emulator. Say what you could not test (real fingertips on the Quest).
- Keep a changelog file sculpt_next_CHANGES.md and list every file you touched.
