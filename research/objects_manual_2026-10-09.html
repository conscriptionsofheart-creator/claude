Your controls, once it's built

Trigger is the index finger. Grip is the middle finger. "Your hand" means your main (dominant) hand.

Built and working now (2026-10-09): see what you will grab (glowing box), grab and carry with the trigger, reach far, push and pull, spin, make it bigger or smaller with both triggers, turn it with both hands, stretch one direction with hands on opposite sides, drop where it is, locked things refuse, undo, move yourself, tap an object for its small menu (Lock, Reset size, Colour on shapes, Duplicate, Delete), pull any of the 28 shapes out of the wrist menu. turn Physics on for an object in its menu and it keeps your hand's speed when you let go, falls, bounces a little and settles on the floor. while its menu is open, handles appear: a cube on each face stretches that side (the far side stays put), an arrow moves it along one axis, a ring turns it about one axis; Snap in the menu makes moves and stretches go in 10 cm steps and turns in 15 degrees. Plastic, Metal and Glow in the menu (solid shapes). stick an object to your hand or your body until you let go, Keep my grip (every grab holds it the way your first did), and a row of 3 or 5 copies along any axis. Passing hand to hand works through the two-hand hold. see-through (100, 75, 50, 25, 10 percent), any body shader on a shape, and join, cut or keep-the-overlap between two shapes, all from the object menu. The wrist menu has an Objects tile (add a shape, pick one of the nearest objects, reopen the last one, Snap, Recently deleted), so nothing needs a tap on the object first. Select several: wrist menu > Objects > Select several, hold the trigger and sweep through the objects you want (a cyan box marks each); the picked menu can keep them together, colour, lock, copy or delete them all, and grabbing any one moves the whole selection or group. Groups last for the session and are not saved with the scene. Put an object in a figure's hand by holding it there and choosing More > Attach to her hand: it follows her wrist when she moves. With Snap on, two-hand scale goes in 10 percent steps. Left out on purpose: a real light inside Glow shapes, because every extra light slows every surface in the headset; Glow already shines on its own (it uses the glow brightness, not a light).

What Horizon Worlds does with objects

Read from Meta's own creator documentation on 9 October 2026 (links at the bottom). Horizon has two modes: Build Mode, where you make things, and play, where things are used.

Build Mode

HighlightPut the tip of the teardrop cursor inside an object. It shows a bounding box.
Grab and move (Grab tool)Hold the index trigger on the highlighted object. It moves with your hand. Holding the trigger in empty space and swiping through objects selects them.

Lock / unlock (Lock tool)Click the trigger on an object to lock it so it can't be edited, moved or deleted; click again to unlock.

One thing Horizon makes you do that you won't have to: switching tools (Grab, Duplicate, Lock, Paint) with the stick, and switching back after, "to avoid accidental duplicates, locking or painting", in Meta's words. Here, grabbing is always on, and the rest live in the object's own menu.

Play mode (grabbable objects)

HoldGrabbable objects are picked up and carried.

Meta's web toolkit for headsets names the same three kinds of grab: one hand (move and turn), two hands (move, turn and scale), and distance (by ray). Only one is active on an object at a time.

What your app does today

  • The trigger poses: it grabs the glowing joint handles on a body.
  • Objects (props, shapes, mirrors, whole bodies) move only while you hold a button on your main hand: the lower button to Move, the upper to Turn, both to Size. Even then, Move only slides, Turn only turns left and right, and Size follows how high your hand goes. That's the part that doesn't feel like a real object.
  • The grip already moves you through the world, as in Horizon. That stays.
  • Locks exist only for joints (the pin). Objects can't be locked yet.
  • Undo and redo already work from the menu hand's stick.

The plan, in the order you'll feel it

Each step leaves everything working, is tested in the headset emulator with measured numbers before you see it, and keeps the old buttons working too.

1

Grab anything, instantly

Hold the trigger on any unlocked object and it's in your hand. It follows your hand's position and its turning, the way a cup does. The stick pushes it away or pulls it in, or spins it. Let go and it stays. One undo per grab.

Test: grab the cone, move the hand 30 cm and turn it 45 degrees, let go. The cone moved 30 cm and turned 45 degrees, and undo puts it back exactly. A joint handle inside the cone still poses the joint.

2

Two hands: size, turn, stretch

Catch the object you're holding with your other trigger too. Hands apart: bigger. Together: smaller. Turn both hands: it turns. Hold it by two opposite sides and pull: it stretches that way only.

Test: hands 40 cm apart moved to 80 cm, the object is exactly twice the size. Hands on the left and right sides pulled apart, only its width changes.

You see it here, after steps 1 and 2.

3

Each object's own menu, and Lock

A quick tap on an object opens its small menu: Lock or Unlock, Duplicate, Delete, Colour, Material (Plastic, Metal, Glow), Physics, Snap, Reset size. Locked objects get a grey outline and a little padlock, and can't be grabbed or deleted. The room, floor and walls start locked; props, shapes and mirrors start unlocked. Locks are saved with the scene. On the desktop, each card gets the same padlock.

4

All the shapes

The new shapes in the section below, as picture cards in four groups. In the headset you pull one straight out of the menu into your hand.

You see it here, after the shapes.

5

Throw and drop

With Physics on, a let-go object keeps the speed and spin of your hand: it flies, falls, bounces a little, and settles on the floor. With Physics off (the default) it stays where you leave it. It's light enough for the headset: no physics engine to download.

6

Copy and delete

Duplicate puts a copy straight into your hand. Delete goes to Recently deleted (More, Recently deleted), and one tap brings it back.

7

Precise handles and snap

Tap an object to show its handles: a box on each face to stretch one side, arrows to slide along one direction, rings to turn about one axis. Snap makes moves 10 cm steps and turns 15 degree steps.

8

Horizon's clever touches, all of them

  • Swipe to select several: hold the trigger in empty space and sweep through objects. Each one you pass through joins the selection, and then they move, size, colour, lock and copy together.
  • Group: in the selection's menu, Group makes them one object you grab as one, and Ungroup splits them again.
  • Copy along a line: hold Duplicate and drag a move arrow. Copies are laid out along that direction at the snap spacing, like a row of stepping stones or a fence.
  • Carry it on you: "Stick to my hand" keeps an object in your hand while you walk and use the menus (a lantern, a flower). "Stick to my body" keeps it at your hip or shoulder.
  • Hand it over: pass an object from one hand to the other by grabbing it with the second hand and letting go with the first. With bodies in the scene, you can place it in a figure's hand (it rides their hand as they move).
  • Where the hand holds it: each object remembers the spot where you first gripped it, so a cup comes up by its handle every time.
  • Glow that lights: the Glow material can also light what's around it, as a small light, if you want.
  • Bounding box and cursor: the glowing box shows exactly which object your hand is on before you grab, so you never pick up the wrong thing.
